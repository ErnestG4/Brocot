# The Map Field

Two layers over the ratio axis, both in closed form, both derived from the
coincidence horizon. This is the display spec for a per-pair overlay on
brocot's map.

Generated by `criticality_tool/cross_substrate/brocot_two_layer_field.py`,
which asserts every claim below and fails its run if any is false.
The field data is `brocot_two_layer_field.json`.

## The two layers

| layer | what it is | shape | drawn as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **REGION** | which below-horizon rational the ratio is *heard as a detuning of* | piecewise constant in α | a fill |
| **EVENT** | where partials merge — a spectral discontinuity | isolated points | a tick |

They do not collide, and that is a theorem rather than a layout choice.

## Why a ratio has a parent at all

Below the horizon — `max(p, q) ≤ 2·order_bound(I)` — two modulators at 1 and
p/q share a sideband exactly, and the spectrum fuses. Above it nothing fuses.
The question that opens is what distinguishes the ratios up there, since every
gross spectral descriptor is identical: at I = 0.9 every node with q ≥ 7 returns
31 partials, top-5 energy 0.716, spectral entropy 0.709.

What distinguishes them is the *closest approach* to a coincidence:

```
gap(α) = min over nonzero |a₁|,|a₂| ≤ A  of  |a₁ + a₂·α|         A = 2·order_bound(I)
```

Exactly 0 below the horizon. Above it, strictly positive — and **physically a
beat rate**: two partials separated by gap·f_c beat at gap·f_c Hz. Measured over
332 ratios in [0.70, 1.40] at I = 0.9, 79.2% of them beat below 20 Hz, which is
inside the range heard as beating or roughness rather than as separate tones.

The pair (a₁, a₂) attaining that minimum names a rational, `|a₁|/|a₂|`, and that
rational is the **parent**. Measured across all 332:

| | |
|---|---|
| the parent is itself below the horizon | **100.0%** |
| the parent is a Stern-Brocot ancestor of α | **100.0%** |
| distinct parents used | 14, the largest holding 34.3% |
| (parent, beat rate to 0.1 Hz) identifies the ratio | 77.7% |

So a ratio above the horizon is heard as *a detuned parent, beating at gap·f_c
Hz*. 39/40 is a detuned 1/1 at 5.5 Hz; 53/40 is a detuned 4/3 at 5.5 Hz; 9/7 is
a detuned 4/3 at 31 Hz — past beating, into separate partials.

### It is not the nearest ancestor

The parent minimises **denominator-weighted** error, `|a₂|·|α − a₁/a₂|`, not
`|α − a₁/a₂|`. A deeper ancestor lies closer in ratio but carries a larger
denominator, and the denominator multiplies the error — because the gap is a
frequency difference between actual partials, and a partial at n₂ = 7 is a
different partial from one at n₂ = 1.

```
α = 19/18,  A = 8
    deepest in-box ancestor  8/7   |7α − 8| = 11/18 = 0.611   →  134 Hz apart
    perceptual parent        1/1   |α  − 1| =  1/18 = 0.056   →  beats at 12.2 Hz
```

19/18 is about a semitone above unison and is heard as a slightly detuned
unison, which is what the argmin says and what any player would say.

**Implementation consequence.** The parent is *not* a prefix of the operator's
stored Stern-Brocot path — that was tested and holds for only 30.1% of ratios —
so the map cannot read it off `PullIndex`. It needs the argmin over the box:
(2A+1)² = 289 integer operations at I = 0.9, computed offline with the rest of
the map fields. Cheap, but not the elegant version, and the difference is worth
stating. The parent *is* always an ancestor, so the path bounds the search.

## Why the layers cannot collide

A below-horizon node is its own parent with gap exactly 0, so a whole
neighbourhood of it takes that node as parent. The argmin can therefore only
switch **strictly between** two nodes, and the boundaries land on their
mediants — 8/9 between 7/8 and 1/1, 9/8 between 1/1 and 8/7, 9/7 between 5/4
and 4/3.

Events sit at region *centres*. Boundaries sit where nothing happens.

This was arrived at the wrong way round and is worth recording as such: the
hypothesis under test was that the audible jumps in a morph *are* the category
changes. It was falsified at 0.0% coverage against 30.4% for the node set, with
the eight largest jumps sitting 1.5 grid steps from a node and 89–140 steps from
any boundary. Nothing merges at a mediant — the parent switches while the
partial set moves continuously. The negative is what gives the display its
cleanest property.

### The field is a Voronoi tessellation

Each region contains **exactly one** event, and that event is the region's own
parent. Asserted at every I, and not designed for: it follows from a node being
its own parent under a nearest-in-the-weighted-metric rule. Every fill is one
tick's basin, never a stripe between two.

## The depth slider subdivides the map

A = 2·order_bound(I), so raising the modulation index moves the horizon out and
splits the field finer:

| I | B | A | regions | events |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.9 | 4 | 8 | 13 | 13 |
| 1.5 | 5 | 10 | 20 | 20 |
| 2.0 | 6 | 12 | 28 | 28 |
| 3.0 | 7 | 14 | 38 | 38 |

Mean truncation depth rises 1.45× from I = 0.9 to I = 3.0. The overlay is not
static; the depth control is one of its two axes.

## Scope — this travels with the feature

* **Two operators.** The coincidence rate among above-horizon parameter sets
  runs 4.7% / 17.3% / 44.5% / 65.9% at N = 2…5 and crosses 0.9 by N = 7
  (validated estimator, `brocot_dissolution_curve`). On a 16-operator patch
  essentially everything coincides somewhere and the categorisation is vacuous.
  **This is a per-pair overlay, not a patch-level one.**
* **The direct (unfolded) channel, two sine modulators, ε = 1e-3** — the
  horizon's own scope, inherited unchanged. See `COINCIDENCE-HORIZON.md`.
* **Beat rate is emitted dimensionless.** Hz is a draw-time conversion against
  the played note: a region beating at 5.5 Hz at f_c = 220 beats at 11 Hz an
  octave up.

## What the display may say

Gated the same way `COINCIDENCE-HORIZON.md` gates its morph wording.

**May say:** "heard as a detuned 4/3", "beats at 5.5 Hz", "fuses" for a node.
The parent is measured at 100% and the beat rate is a frequency difference
between predicted partials.

**May not say:** that the regions are equally spaced perceptually — they are
not, and four attempts to linearise the morph across them failed on two
different coordinates. Nor that a region boundary is audible as an event; it is
specifically the place where nothing happens.

**Unresolved:** the node set accounts for only 30.4% of large timbral jumps on
a morph path. The remaining ~70% are amplitude-threshold crossings, where a
partial crosses the −50 dB floor at ratios no Farey set enumerates. A complete
event layer needs an amplitude scan the horizon does not supply.
